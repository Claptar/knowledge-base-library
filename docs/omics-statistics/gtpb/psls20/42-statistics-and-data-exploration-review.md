# 42. Statistics and Data Exploration Review

## What this covers

This chapter is the first wrap-up session of the course, placed right after the material on
experimental design and on the basic concepts of statistics. It introduces nothing new: it
revisits the three strands of Part 1 — experimental design, data exploration, and statistical
inference — and turns each one into a question the reader should be able to answer about their own
understanding. It assumes the lectures on experimental design, on random variables and estimation,
and on data exploration that came before it.

## What Part 1 covered

The wrap-up organises Part 1 of the course into three strands:

- Experimental design
- Data exploration
- Statistical inference

### Experimental design

The concepts singled out for review:

- Defining the population under study
- The importance of randomisation
- Sample size
- Control: simultaneous control groups, randomising, and blinding
- Confounding — and recognising which kinds of studies carry the higher risk of it

### Basic concepts of statistics

- Types of variables
- Random variables
- Probability theory
- Statistical modelling
- Estimation
- Why every statistic or estimator computed from data is itself a random variable
- The distinction between an empirical approach and one that rests on distributional assumptions

### Data exploration

The wrap-up names data exploration as the third strand of Part 1 but does not add further material
of its own under this heading — the closing questions on showing data raw and on spotting
departures from normality are what stand in for it here.

## Questions to check your own understanding

The wrap-up closes with five questions, used to gauge confidence topic by topic rather than to
grade it. The point is to answer them honestly to yourself before moving on, not to score them:

1. How confident are you in recognising different types of variables in a study?
2. How confident are you in understanding the importance of randomisation?
3. How confident are you in understanding how data, statistics and conclusions change from sample
   to sample, and what the impact of sample size is on that uncertainty?
4. How confident are you in explaining to somebody else the importance of data exploration, and
   the importance of showing the data as raw as possible?
5. How confident are you in spotting data that deviates from the normal distribution?
