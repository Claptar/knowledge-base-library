# 15. Quorum Sensing and Cell-Cell Communication

## What this covers

This is the transition slide set into the course's last unit: from a single cell treated on its
own to a multicellular organism built from cells that talk to one another. The only source for
this chapter is a slide deck (converted from a PDF with no text layer, its figures removed for
copyright), so the chapter is short and stays close to what those slide headings actually assert.
It assumes the earlier framing of the course — single-cell systems biology, both at the level of
the whole cell as a reactor and at the level of diffusion and gradients inside it — and does not
attempt to reconstruct any mechanism the slides only showed as a picture.

## Three levels of systems biology

The slides frame the course's material as three nested questions, moving from a single cell in
isolation to a population of cells that must coordinate:

- **Systems microbiology** treats the single cell as a well-stirred bio-reactor.
- **Systems cell biology** stays with the single cell but now takes diffusion and spatial
  gradients seriously, since these matter for regulation inside the cell.
- **Systems developmental biology** asks how a **multicellular** organism is built from single
  cells — which requires **cell-cell communication**.

This chapter's material sits at the start of the third of these: it introduces the experimental
system the course uses to study cell-cell communication.

## Quorum sensing as the experimental system

The slides name bacterial **quorum sensing** as "a tractable experimental system for cell-cell
communication," and identify **N-acyl-L-homoserine lactones (AHLs)** as the main signalling
molecules in that system. Beyond those two facts, the slides carry no reconstructable content
here: the explanatory figures for both headings were removed from the converted source for
copyright reasons, and there is no accompanying transcript to describe what they showed. So this
chapter can record that quorum sensing and AHL signalling are the system chosen for study, but not
the mechanism — the threshold behaviour, the diffusion argument, or whatever diagram made the case
for "tractable" — since none of that survived the conversion.

## An engineered example: programmed population control

The slides point to a specific paper as "an example of engineering cell-cell communication":

> You, L., R. S. Cox 3rd, R. Weiss, and F. H. Arnold. "Programmed population control by
> cell-cell communication and regulated killing." *Nature* 428, no. 6985 (22 April 2004): 868–71.

As with the two headings above, the figure illustrating the circuit was removed from the converted
slides, and there is no transcript describing it. The pointer itself — that a synthetic
cell-cell-communication circuit can be built to regulate a population's size by coupling
communication to killing — is everything this material supports; the circuit's design and how it
achieves that regulation are not recoverable from what was supplied and should be read from the
paper directly.
