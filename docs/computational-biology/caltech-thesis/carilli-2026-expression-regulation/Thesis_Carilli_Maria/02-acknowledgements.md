Firstly, I would like to thank to my advisor, Lior Pachter, whose kindness, wisdom, and passion for the truth were bright beacons throughout my graduate school journey. I will be forever amazed at his ability to provide the space and encouragement to let so many personalities, interests, and ideas flourish uniquely. He showed me how important it is to be a good and principled scientist; and as important to be a good and principled person.

I would like to thank also my committee chair, Rob Phillips, whose enthusiasm for science and sense of wonder leave an indelible mark. Few days go by that I don’t look about the world and wonder at its working because of the philosophy I’ve learned from him. Thank you also to my committee members Yisong Yue and Barbara Engelhardt, who have provided thoughtful scientific guidance and advice on the longer-term prospects of career and life.

Over the course my Ph.D., I have had the immense privilege to work with and learn from curious and creative collaborators and mentors, whom I also call friends. I especially thank Gennady Gorin for his unconvential vision for what single-cell data analysis should be. I am thankful for his mentorship, discussions, and availability during and after his time at Caltech. I would also like to thank Ingilief B. Hallgrímsdóttir for introducing me to the world of genetics: I learned from her experience and insight at our every meeting and conversation. Thank you to Kayla Jackson, for companionship through tough classes and whose close collaboration on our recent project reminded me how enriching and enjoyable the pursuit of knowledge can be. Thank you to Tara Chari, for advice on projects and the ups and downs of grad school.

Thank you to all of the members of the Pachter lab, past and present, who provided an intellectually rich and personally supportive community in which to work: Laura Luebbert, Delaney Sullivan, Meichen Fang, Nikhila Swarna, Conrad Oakes, Joe Rich, Maya Caskey, Charlene Kim, Vera Beilenson, Catherine Felce, Rebekah Loving Ngo, Taleen Dilanyan, Anne Yeokyoung Kil, Kristján Eldjárn Hjörleifsson, Lambda Moses, Sina Booeshaghi. Thank you also to the members of the Mortazavi lab, particularly Ryan Weber.

I have deeply appreciated my years as part of the close-knit and brilliant Caltech community. Particularly, thank you to Tom Röschinger who, as my mentor for my

iv

first-year first rotation, made my entry into graduate school smooth and fun; and to Justin Bois, who is an example of what a great teacher should be. I also thank Elijah Cole from whom I learned about doing research in industry during a summer internship.

I am thankful for the mentorship of Deborah Wuttke, my undergraduate advisor, for her support and belief in me: without it I would never have come to Caltech.

Beyond the academic, thank you to Nikhila Swarna, Juliet Lee, and Samantha Chang, for our LA explorations, music making, and many girls’ nights.

These past five years, I have run more miles than there are pages in this thesis (by at least one order of magnitude). For being companions on these adventurous endeavors on the trails of Southern California, I would like to my friends in the Caltech Alpine Club. I wish I could list all of their names here, but if I tried, I’d be writing all night, and I have to get for the 7 a.m. trail run.

I most especially want to thank my family, to have as which I am the luckiest person in the world. Michael, Joe and Tina, John and Kelsey, and my sweetest nieces, Kaia and Nika. They may be my siblings, but they are awfully cool humans. Thank you to my sister, Rose, to whom I could (and do) talk for hours and still have words for hours more. Though we’ve been on opposite coasts, the stresses of graduate school and the weights of life have been lessened by her friendship.

And, of course, my thanks to Jackson Owen Parker, my “shelter from the storm,” for his constancy, his humor, and his goodness of heart.

Finally, I would like to thank my parents, Christopher and Adrianna Carilli, for filling our home with books and our heads with questions. Thank you is a pale phrase to offer in light of their sacrifices, originality, and love.
