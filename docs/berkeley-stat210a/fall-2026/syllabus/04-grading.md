# Grading

**Source:** [`syllabus.qmd`](https://github.com/berkeley-stat210a/fall-2026/blob/7dc8f80da94dff74532d9e3923afdb16a255262d/syllabus.qmd) · **Licence:** CC BY 4.0 · Converted 2026-09-14 from `.qmd` (lossless)

Your final grade is based on:

-   Homework completion: 10%

-   Tutorial attendance: 10%

-   Tutorial solution correctness: 20%

-   Weekly quizzes: 20%

-   Final exam: 40%

**Weekly homework (ungraded):** Each week, there will be a problem set with four problems, released on Wednesday, and due by 11:59pm the following Wednesday, with solutions released 15 minutes after. You are welcome to work with each other or consult articles, textbooks, or generative AI, but you are **strongly recommended** to write it up yourself without help.

**Tuesday Quizzes:** At the beginning of class each Tuesday, beginning September 8, there will be a 10-minute quiz consisting of a short question covering material in a recent lecture. Your quiz grade will be based on your highest 9 quiz scores. We cannot offer makeups for missed quizzes.

**Tutorial Sections (new this semester):** Beginning the week of September 14, every student will attend a recurring one-hour weekly small-group tutorial section, with mandatory attendance, framed around discussion the previous week's homework and course material. You should come prepared to solve problems from the previous week's homework, either orally on the board or on paper, without notes. For oral presentations, you may be asked questions about steps as you go. Your odds of scoring well will be higher if you have read and understood the solutions. Your grade will be based on your best $8 + \lceil n/2\rceil$ solutions, where $n$ is the number of times you are called on.

**Excused absence policy:** If you need to miss a tutorial, use the [excused absence form]() to explain why. These are intended to cover eventualities like excused absences, medical issues, and emergencies. If you have no more than than two absences, they will be granted with minimal scrutiny; more absences require more justification.

**Academic integrity:** You are expected to abide by the [Berkeley honor code](http://teaching.berkeley.edu/berkeley-honor-code). Violating the collaboration policy, or cheating in any other way, will result in a failing grade for the semester and you will be reported to the University Office of Student Conduct.
