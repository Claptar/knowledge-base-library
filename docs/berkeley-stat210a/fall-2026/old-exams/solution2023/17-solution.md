This follows from the continuous mapping theorem after observing that _X → µ_ and _Y → ν_ in probability, by the law of large numbers, and the function _f_ ( _x, y_ ) = ( _x/y_ )<sup>2</sup> is continuous everywhere except where _y_ = 0.

**Common “error”:** The function _f_ is not continuous everywhere. If you said or implied in your answer that the function _f_ was continuous, without the caveat that the denominator must be nonzero (i.e. that _ν >_ 0), I deducted 1 point out of 5. I made exceptions in rare cases where it seemed that something else in the answer made implicit reference to _ν >_ 0 being necessary.
