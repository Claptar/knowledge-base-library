# 19. Course Overview and Schedule

## What this covers

This chapter is not a single lecture's mathematics — it is the map of the term that the course's
own home pages lay out. It answers two questions a reader needs answered before opening any
lecture in this course: what kind of course Statistics 210A is and who it is for, and in what
order the theory is built up across a semester of lectures, recitations, homework, and the final
exam. It assumes nothing beyond wanting to know what lies ahead; no probability or statistics
content is developed here, since none is given in the source pages — only the schedule of it.

## What the course is

Stat 210A is UC Berkeley's introductory Ph.D.-level course in theoretical statistics. The course's
own description is blunt about its pace: it is "a fast-paced and demanding course intended to
prepare students for research careers in statistics." It is offered every fall — the department's
site shows Fall 2024, Fall 2025 and Fall 2026 iterations — with the same overall shape repeating
from year to year.

The course is aimed at incoming statistics Ph.D. students. Undergraduates who want to take it are
asked to fill out a permission-code request form describing their background, and are pointed to a
set of frequently-asked-questions covering preparation and review material; that background review
is referred to by the home page but not included in it.

## How a term runs

Three platforms carry the logistics: a syllabus page for basic course information, Gradescope for
turning in homework, EdStem for announcements and technical discussion — explicitly *not* the place
for homework spoilers — and bCourses for lecture videos and homework solutions.

Within a typical week the rhythm is: one or two lectures, each followed by a posted set of
handwritten notes; roughly one new homework set assigned per week (twelve across the Fall 2025
term); and, near the start of the term, a recitation section reviewing the probability background
the course assumes. The term ends with a final exam. None of the homework problems, handwritten
notes, or exam questions themselves are part of this chapter's source material — the home pages
only link to them.

## The sequence of topics across the term

The Fall 2025 schedule is the most complete of the three home pages read for this chapter, and its
week-by-week lecture list is the clearest record of how the course orders its material. Grouping
consecutive weeks by the topic they visit (a grouping made for readability here, not a division the
source itself names) gives the following arc:

**Weeks 1–2 — orientation.** Lecture 1, Introduction; Lecture 2, Probability; Lecture 3,
Estimation; a recitation reviewing probability.

**Weeks 3–4 — sufficiency and exponential families.** Lecture 4, Sufficiency; Lecture 5,
Exponential Families (given a second lecture the following week); Lecture 7, Completeness.

**Weeks 5–8 — the estimation-theory core.** Lecture 8, Unbiased Estimation; Lecture 9, Score and
Fisher Information; Lecture 10, Bayes Estimation; Lecture 11, Interpretation and Choice of Prior;
Lecture 12, Bayesian Computation; Lecture 13, the James–Stein Estimator; Lecture 14, Minimax
Estimation.

**Weeks 8–11 — hypothesis testing.** Lecture 15, Hypothesis Testing; Lecture 16, Testing with One
Parameter; Lecture 17, p-values and Confidence Intervals; Lecture 18, Testing with Nuisance
Parameters; Lectures 19–20, Testing in Linear Models (two lectures).

**Weeks 11–13 — asymptotics and likelihood.** Lecture 21, Asymptotics; Lecture 22, Maximum
Likelihood Estimation; Lecture 23, MLE Consistency; Lecture 24, Likelihood-Based Inference.

**Weeks 14–15 — resampling and multiple comparisons.** Lecture 25, Bootstrap; Lecture 26, Multiple
Testing (given across two class meetings).

**Week 17 — final exam**, 3–6pm.

Twelve homework sets are interleaved through weeks 3–12, each posted the week after the lectures it
draws on. The Fall 2025 term's second home page (served from a `units/` path on the same site)
records the same course but only as far as Homework 1 in week 3, with slightly different Gradescope
and EdStem course numbers than the main page — evidently an earlier snapshot of the same offering
rather than a different course. The Fall 2026 home page, read at conversion time, carries the same
course description and the same four resource links (syllabus, Gradescope, EdStem, bCourses) but no
populated schedule yet — its schedule section was still an empty placeholder.
