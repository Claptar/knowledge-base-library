For each of the following situations, give the response, factors, units, and type of design. For each factor, indicate whether it is experimental/observation/blocking and the number of levels. Describe the potential outcomes and how many there are for each unit.

1. Researchers want to study how phone notification settings affect students’ focus during study sessions. One setting they consider is frequency (high vs. low); the second is tone (sound on vs. vibrate). 24 students are randomly assigned to a level of both frequency and tone, with an equal number of students in each group. Each student studies for 45 minutes while their phone receives simulated messages. The outcome is the number of correctly answered questions on a quiz administered immediately afterwards.

2. A public policy researcher is analyzing how commuting method and income level are associated with well-being in a sample of 600 Bay Area residents. Participants are classified by commuting method (car, public transit, bicycle, or walk) and income category (low, medium, high). The outcome is their score on a 10-item subjective well-being scale. The researcher uses existing survey data.

3. Public health researchers draft a script of helpful questions for doctors to ask patients during interactions in the clinic. Six of the 12 doctors practicing in a single facility are chosen at random to receive copies of the script and a brief training about how to use it. The next day, each of the 12 doctors sees 8 patients and treats them all according to the script. The public health researchers interview each of the 96 patients immediately after their visit and have each patient rate their satisfaction with the care they received on a scale from 1 to 5.
