Researchers at UC Berkeley are studying whether AI chatbots can be tuned to increase students’ understanding of an academic topic. They plan to recruit and compensate 40 UC Berkeley students to participate in a one-hour lesson. In the first 15 minutes each student learns about a new statistical topic either by reading about it or by watching a live lecturer. That is followed by 25 minutes where they work on a handout with practice problems, during which they are encouraged to use one of two AI chatbots. In the final 20 minutes they are given a quiz that they take without the chatbot. Their quiz score out of 100 serves as the measure of the student’s understanding.

One of the chatbots is an answerer-AI that behaves like ChatGPT: if you ask it a question, it will provide a complete answer. The other chatbot is a teacher-AI that, when asked a question, behaves more similarly to a human teacher: it doesn’t immediately provide an answer but asks the student to explain what they know and asks follow-up questions.

Each student will be randomly assigned to either be taught via reading or lecture; and to interact either with a teacher-AI or an answerer-AI; with an equal number of students in each group.

Once the researchers began to write their protocol, they realized there was a constraint: they didn’t have the time or space to study each student individually. Instead, they decided to direct students to sign up for one of 4 sessions, each taking place in a classroom with 10 total students. Each classroom will be randomly assigned to receive the lesson either via live lecture or through printed readings. As before, each individual student in the class will be randomly assigned either to get the teacher-AI or the answerer-AI, five students assigned to each.
