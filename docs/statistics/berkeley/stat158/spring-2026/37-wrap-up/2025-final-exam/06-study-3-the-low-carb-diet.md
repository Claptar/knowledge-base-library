A researcher enrolls 3,000 subjects in a study and randomly assigns 1,000 of them to eat a low carbohydrate diet for a month. The remaining 2,000 are given no dietary guidance (the control). At the end of the month, researchers measure whether each subject can run a mile in a prespecified time. When subjects are tested at the end of the month, 500 out of the 1,000 subjects in the treated group report that they ate the diet; however, in reality only 250 of them did so. The results of the experiment show that 400 out of 1,000 subjects assigned to treatment ran the mile within the prespecified time period, and that 700 out of the 2,000 subjects assigned to control did so.

17. If instead you discovered that only 250 subjects had eaten the diet, what would your new estimate of the CACE be?
