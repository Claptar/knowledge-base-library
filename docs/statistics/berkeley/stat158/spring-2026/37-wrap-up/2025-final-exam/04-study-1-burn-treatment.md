During WWII in England, men volunteered to be part of research to investigate how to best treat burns from possible chemical gas attacks from Germany. The volunteers agreed to be given small burns with mustard gas to assess what treatment of victims would be most beneficial. The statistical analysis of this work was overseen by a researcher, George E. P. Box (1919-2013), who was to become a preeminent statistician in the area of experimental design.

Six burn medications (including the control of no medication) were tested. Burns heal differently on different parts of the body, so six sites on the arms were considered. After each medication was applied, the burn was covered by a clean gauze; the response was the number of hours for a clearly defined degree of partial healing to occur.

Consider the following completely randomized design: the combination of burn medications (6 levels) and locations (6 levels) yields 36 treatment combinations. Each volunteer will be randomly assigned one of these 36 treatment combinations: a burn will be created on the volunteer at the site assigned and then the assigned treatment will be applied to the burn.
