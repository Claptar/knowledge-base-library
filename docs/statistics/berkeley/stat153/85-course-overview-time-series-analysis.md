# 85. Course Overview: Time Series Analysis

## What this covers

The five supplied files are the public home pages of Berkeley's Stat 153, one per semester it has
run from Fall 2024 through Fall 2026. None of them contains lecture slides, a transcript, or
problem-set text — each is a logistics page: instructor and GSI contacts, meeting times, and links
out to a syllabus and a schedule that are not part of the supplied material. This chapter is
therefore an orientation to the course itself, not to a topic in time series analysis: what the
course claims to cover, how it is run, and who has run it. Nothing about trends, ARMA models,
forecasting or spectra is developed further than the one-paragraph description below, because
nothing further than that was in the source.

## What the course covers

Only one of the five pages — the Spring 2026 offering, cross-listed as Stat 248 — states the
course's scope directly:

> An introduction to time series analysis in the time domain and spectral domain. Topics will
> include: estimation of trends and seasonal effects, autoregressive moving average models,
> forecasting, indicators, harmonic analysis, spectra.

The same page states that the course uses Python as its primary computing language. The other four
home pages do not repeat this description; they list only staff, meeting times, and links to a
syllabus and schedule.

The course's catalogue number pairs with a graduate-level number, Stat 248, in two of the five
offerings (Fall 2026's page is titled "STAT 153 - STAT 248: Time Series Analysis"; Spring 2026's is
titled "Stat 153 / Stat 248: Introduction to Time Series"). The earlier three offerings (Fall 2024,
Spring 2025, Fall 2025) carry no such cross-listing in their titles.

## Format: lecture and lab

Two of the five pages give meeting times explicitly:

- **Fall 2024**: lecture Tuesdays and Thursdays, 2-3:30pm (McCone 141); lab Fridays, in two
  two-hour sections (9-11am and 1-3pm, both in Evans Hall).
- **Spring 2026**: lecture Tuesdays and Thursdays, 8-9:30am (VLSB 2060); lab Fridays, in four
  two-hour sections spread across the morning and afternoon (Evans Hall).

Both therefore describe the same pattern: two lectures a week plus a weekly lab, run in several
parallel sections to accommodate enrollment. The Spring 2025, Fall 2025 and Fall 2026 home pages do
not list class or lab times on the page itself.

## What isn't here

Every home page points further than it contains. Fall 2024 links to a GitHub repository holding the
source files for lectures and homeworks, an Ed Discussion forum, and bCourses for grading; all five
pages link to a syllabus page and a schedule that in four of the five cases is rendered from an
external `schedule.yml` file. None of that linked content — the week-by-week topics, the readings,
the homework assignments, or the syllabus text itself — was included in the supplied material, so
none of it is reconstructed here. A chapter on the actual mathematics of trend estimation, ARMA
models, or spectral analysis as this course teaches it would need those files as input.
