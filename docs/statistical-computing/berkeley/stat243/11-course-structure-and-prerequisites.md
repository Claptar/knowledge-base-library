# 11. Course Structure and Prerequisites

## What this covers

This chapter is not a lecture on a computing technique. The material supplied for it is the course
home page of Statistics 243, *Introduction to Statistical Computing*, as it appeared in three
successive years (fall 2024, fall 2025, fall 2026). It answers a different question from the rest
of the notes: not "how do you compute X" but "what is this course, how is it organised, and what
does it assume of you before the first technical unit starts." It assumes nothing beyond an
intention to take, or evaluate, the course.

## What the course is

Statistics 243 is an introduction to statistical computing. In fall 2024 and fall 2025 it ran as a
single semester-long course. By fall 2026 it had split into two half-semester courses, Stat 243A
and Stat 243B, taught back to back under one shared course website; policies and logistics are
described as pertaining to both, "with limited exceptions."

Course content — unit notes, labs, and problem sets — is distributed through the course website
rather than through the home page itself, reached via the site's sidebar navigation.

## Who can take it, and in what combination

Registration is gated by department: graduate Statistics students have first claim, and only once
they have had the opportunity to register does the instructor consider undergraduates or students
from other departments; concurrent-enrollment students (for example, exchange students from other
institutions) are considered on the same "once Berkeley students have had their chance" basis.
Anyone auditing, or otherwise not enrolled through the normal path, is asked to identify themselves
through a survey early in the term.

Once the course became a 243A/243B pair (fall 2026), a further choice opened up: a student need not
take both halves. Two constraints apply to that choice:

- A student who wants **243B without 243A** needs to already be comfortable programming in Python —
  not necessarily to the depth 243A itself teaches, but enough to not be learning the language and
  the statistical-computing content at the same time.
- A Statistics MA student who wants to skip one half of the pair — rather than an outside student
  who was only ever going to take one — needs a waiver from the MA program, and has to demonstrate
  mastery of the material in the half being skipped.

The prerequisites for 243A and 243B are explicitly not the same as each other; the home page defers
the detail to the syllabus rather than stating it.

## What it expects on day one, and the first three weeks

Two kinds of background are expected coming in: a math/statistics background (specified in the
syllabus, not on the home page itself) and familiarity with Python — or, failing that, a
willingness to get up to speed in it quickly. The course does not assume the second is already in
place: the opening weeks are explicitly built to bring everyone up to a common level in the bash
shell and in Python at the same time, and are described, across all three years of the home page,
as involving "a lot of moving pieces." The practical instruction that follows from this is to track
the course schedule closely during that stretch rather than to arrive already fluent.
